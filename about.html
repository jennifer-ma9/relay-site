Why I built Relay

Written by Jennifer Ma, founder of Relay

When I was a freshman in high school, I joined a club at my school where we would tutor girls in South Africa. Every Wednesday morning, we'd join a Zoom call with 10–14 girls who shared one computer, and work through math and science problems with them. This became one of my favorite clubs, and I hoped to eventually become a head of the organization.

However, the next year, the founder and Senior Head of our club graduated, and we lost contact with the South African school. I wondered for months after this, what would happen to the routine we'd built with the South African girls. Their weekly tutoring and improvement had been stalled and abandoned completely. The community we had been helping still existed, their need for our help still existed, everything – lesson plans, tutors, were still in place – yet the club simply disappeared because there was no one to take it over.

I've since watched similar things happen to other student-led projects. Ones with more pressing issues like food insecurity, clothing insecurity, and health.

Relay is an attempt to pass on existing projects, keeping the torch of passion and impact alive.
