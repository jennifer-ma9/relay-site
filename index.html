A registry for student-led projects in transition

Most youth nonprofits don't fail. They just lose their founder.

Relay is a handoff registry: outgoing student founders record what their project actually needs to keep running, and incoming students take it over with the contacts, budget, and playbook already in hand — instead of starting from zero, or letting the work quietly stop.

Why Relay exists
"We tutored the same group of girls in math and science every Wednesday morning. Then the club head graduated, and the program just... disappeared."

That's how this started — a tutoring program for girls in South Africa that ran for a year on shared laptops and a weekly video call, built by students who cared and who eventually left for college. The girls didn't stop needing tutors. The lesson plans, the contact who coordinated the classroom, the schedule that worked — none of that vanished because the students weren't willing. It vanished because there was nowhere for it to go.

Relay exists so the next student who wants to start "a tutoring nonprofit" can instead pick up one that already has a classroom, a contact, and a Wednesday slot — and so the communities being served aren't the ones absorbing the cost of a college application cycle ending.

How it works

A handoff, not a hand-me-down

Relay isn't a legal transfer of a nonprofit's assets — that stays with founders and their boards. It's a structured record of what makes a project work, so continuity doesn't depend on one person's memory.

01

Founders record the playbook

Outgoing leaders fill out a continuity record: who's involved, what the weekly rhythm looks like, budget, contacts, what worked and what didn't.

02

Incoming leaders apply

Students looking to lead something apply to specific projects, not a blank page — with a short written commitment to the community already being served.

03

A supervised handoff period

Outgoing and incoming leaders overlap for a set window — shadowing sessions, warm introductions to contacts — before the record marks the project as transferred.
