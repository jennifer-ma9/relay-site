Backstory
"We tutored the same group of girls in math and science every Wednesday morning. Then the club head graduated, and the program just... disappeared."

That was a tutoring program for girls in South Africa — a year of shared laptops and a weekly video call, run by students who genuinely cared. When the person leading it left for college, nobody else picked it up. The girls still needed tutors. The lesson plans still existed. The contact at their school was still there. None of it disappeared because we stopped caring — it disappeared because there was no one left holding it, and no clear way to hand it to someone who could.

I've watched the same thing happen to other clubs since — a new one starts almost every year, and an old one quietly stops. Relay is my attempt at breaking that cycle: instead of a new student starting from nothing, they can pick up a project that already has a classroom, a contact, and a Wednesday time slot.

How it works

What actually happens when you list a project

Relay doesn't touch anything legal — your nonprofit's paperwork, bank account, and board stay exactly where they are. What we're trying to hand off is everything that usually only lives in the founder's head.

01

Write down how it actually runs

Who's involved, what a normal week looks like, what it costs, who to call, and what you'd tell the next person if you had ten minutes with them.

02

Someone applies to take it over

They're applying to run this specific project, not just expressing general interest — and they say a little about why and what they can commit to.

03

You overlap before it's official

You stay involved for a stretch — showing them the ropes, introducing them to your contacts — before you actually step back.
