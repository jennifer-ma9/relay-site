FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How can Relay be used by students?

Relay serves two primary groups of students: those preparing to graduate and pass on projects they have built, and those looking to lead a cause, organization, or initiative that aligns with their interests. Relay connects these students to make the transition between generations more seamless.

For incoming leaders, Relay provides access to the practical knowledge needed to get started — from budgets, member counts, and organizational structures to past meeting materials, resources, and examples of successful programming. For outgoing leaders, Relay offers peace of mind that the work they invested in will not simply disappear when they graduate, but can continue under a new generation of leadership.

By preserving these connections and institutional knowledge, Relay also creates opportunities for collaboration, mentorship, and networking among student leaders across generations.

How can Relay be used by schools?

Relay helps schools streamline and preserve the life cycles of student organizations and initiatives.

When a club is first chartered, schools often collect valuable information such as its mission, constitution, budget, membership numbers, and organizational structure. However, when a club becomes inactive, that institutional knowledge can easily become difficult to access — even when the underlying idea remains valuable.

For example, a Debate Club may become dormant after several years, only for a new group of students to propose starting a nearly identical organization. If the school's existing club records were preserved on Relay, students could discover the organization's history, resources, and previous structure before creating something from scratch.

Schools could also use Relay as a centralized archive for student organizations, allowing administrators and students to identify existing or dormant organizations, preserve institutional knowledge, and build upon previous work rather than repeatedly starting from zero.

What if I want to "found" a club even though a similar club already exists?

Relay believes that leadership is about more than being the first person to have an idea.

If a similar organization already exists — or existed in the past — there may still be meaningful opportunities to take the project in a new direction. Instead of discarding the work of previous students, a new leader can build upon that foundation, introduce their own vision, and create the next chapter of the organization's history.

Consider: 2025 — Founded by Maya. 2026 — Expanded to three schools. 2027 — Leadership transferred to Sarah. 2028 — Sarah launched a new mentorship program.

Sarah did not technically "found" the organization. But she expanded its reach, introduced a new initiative, and left her own mark on its development. Her leadership and impact are no less meaningful because someone else started the project.

Relay therefore treats project lineage as an asset, not a limitation. A project can have multiple generations of leaders, each contributing something distinct to its growth and evolution. Rather than asking "Who founded it?", Relay asks: "What did you do with it?"

What should I do if the organization I am looking to pass on is school-based?

If you are open to expanding your organization beyond your school — whether to other schools in your region or to students more broadly — you can create a regular Relay listing and note that the organization originated within a school environment.

If you would like to keep the organization exclusively school-based, select your affiliated school when registering. If your school already has a Relay profile, your organization can be connected to its school portal.

If your school does not yet have a Relay profile, please contact both Relay and an appropriate school administrator so that we can help establish one.

What if I can't find an existing project that matches what I want to do?

That may be a good thing.

Relay is designed not only to preserve existing projects, but to help students build new ones. If you have a vision for an initiative that does not yet exist on Relay — or even in your community — it may be an opportunity to create something genuinely new.

You can use Relay to develop your idea, find potential collaborators, and build a team around your vision. And when your time with the organization eventually comes to an end, you can pass it forward to the next generation rather than letting the work disappear.

Not every idea needs a predecessor. But every great project can have a successor.
